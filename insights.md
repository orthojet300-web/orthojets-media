## What works / what doesn't
- **Patient-scenario hook + a decision question wins on both networks.** Best TikTok: cauda equina ("Back pain + numb
  saddle + bladder change") 371 and Gartland ("Child's elbow fracture: cast or pins?") 178 with 13 likes. Best IG ever:
  fat embolism ("Day 2 after a femur fracture, hypoxic and confused") 187 views, 4 likes, 4.1 s avg watch.
- **A classification framed as a decision works**: Gartland "cast or pins?" beat every other classification
  (Gustilo 132, Salter-Harris 117). Keep framing classifications around the one decision they change.
- **Score / number-first hooks are weakest**: MESS 7 (121 TikTok, 0 IG saves), Salter-Harris 117. A named score in the
  first line ("MESS 7: amputate?") does not stop the scroll as well as a patient in one line.
- **Series (TikTok avg views):** Don't Miss 214 (cauda equina lifts it), Classified 142, Clinical Rules 140, Ortho
  Numbers 134. On Instagram Classified leads (127), Numbers and Rules ~109.
- Instagram hold improved this week (2.3–4.5 s vs 1.3–3.6 s): the patient-first hooks with movement in the first second help.
  Still under 10% of a 60 s reel, so the first 2 s remain the main lever.
- Engagement: likes are the only reliable signal. A/B questions still give 0 real comments; saves 0–1 per reel.
- Music: both top TikTok episodes (cauda equina, Gartland) used `user_track2_calm_tiktok`; every other track sits in the
  115–148 band. Weak signal (n=2), not a reason to change rotation yet. Visual theme: no visible effect.

## Guidance for the next episodes
- Line 1 (= first caption line + TikTok title + hook card): the patient in one line (age/mechanism + alarming sign) +
  a short decision question. E.g. "Swollen finger after a cat bite, can't straighten it: wash out tonight?".
  Name the sign/score/classification only after that. Avoid opening with a score name or a statistic.
- First 1–2 s: movement + the key visual (X-ray / diagram), no title card.
